Recent Research

Takings for Economic Development: An Empirical Analysis of Eminent Domain Exercises in New York City
Ronit Levine-Schnur, Gary A. Wagner
Journal of Empirical Legal Studies, Forthcoming
Across nearly three decades of property takings in New York City, we find little evidence that eminent domain boosts nearby jobs, businesses, or property values, and where effects show up they are more often negative than positive.
Net Productive Entrepreneurship: State and MSA-Level Indices, 2002–2019
Justin T. Callais, Peter T. Calcagno, Gary A. Wagner
Applied Economics Letters, 2026
New yearly measures of productive and unproductive entrepreneurship for every state and 381 metro areas show that net productive entrepreneurship has declined since 2002, mostly in large metros.
The Political Economy of State Economic Development: A Path to Unproductive Entrepreneurship
Russell S. Sobel, Gary A. Wagner, Peter T. Calcagno
Small Business Economics, 2026
After a state hands out its first extraordinarily large business incentive, jobs at existing lobbying firms grow by 3.6%, with the biggest gains in the state capital.
Baumol’s migrants: Productive and unproductive entrepreneurship and between-MSA migration
Justin T. Callais, Vincent Geloso, Alicia Plemmons, Gary A. Wagner
European Journal of Political Economy, 2025
Americans move toward metro areas where entrepreneurs innovate and away from those where entrepreneurs chase political favors.
Institutions Matter: Economic freedom and income mobility
Justin T. Callais, Vincent J. Geloso, Alicia M. Plemmons, Gary A. Wagner
Economic Modelling, 2025
Children who grow up in U.S. metro areas with more economic freedom are more likely to climb the income ladder, with 5 to 12% more upward mobility than children in the least-free areas.
Evaluation of Kelo’s Political and Economic Impact: Theory and Evidence
Ronit Levine-Schnur, Gary A. Wagner
Yale Journal on Regulation, 2025
After the Supreme Court’s Kelo decision, New York City cut its economic-development takings by about 90% without any new law forcing it to, and the takings it did carry out produced no measurable gains in local jobs or businesses.
Short-Term Rental Bans and the Hotel Industry: Evidence from New York City
Sebastian C. Anastasi, Alexander Marsella, Vitor Melo, E. Frank Stephenson, Gary A. Wagner
European Journal of Political Economy, 2025
New York City’s de facto ban on short-term rentals raised hotel room rates by $14 to $19 a night and added roughly $2.1 to $2.9 billion in hotel revenue over its first eighteen months.
Employment Effects of Minimum Wage Indexing: Establishment Evidence from Oregon Restaurants
Stephen Miller, Gary A. Wagner, Alicia Plemmons
Economic Inquiry, 2025
After Oregon tied its minimum wage to inflation in 2003, employment at its restaurants fell about 3.6% relative to similar restaurants in states where the minimum wage stayed put.
Relationship Lobbying through Repeated Contributions: A Humanomics Approach
David Mitchell, Todd Nesbit, Gary A. Wagner
Public Choice, 2025
When state term limits pushed long-serving legislators out and shifted power toward governors, the share of campaign money that statewide executive candidates got from repeat donors rose by about a third, a sign that political influence is built through lasting relationships.
Cashing in on Culture: Local Employment Effects from Art and Cultural District Designation
Gary A. Wagner, Javier E. Portillo
Journal of Cultural Economics, 2024
State-designated cultural districts give a modest but lasting boost to jobs at arts and cultural businesses inside their borders, but the gains do not spread to restaurants, shops, or hotels.
Labor Market Reform as an External Enabler of High-Growth Entrepreneurship: A Multi-Level Institutional Contingency Perspective
Daniel L. Bennett, Gary A. Wagner, Michael Araki
Journal of Business Venturing, 2024
When states made noncompete agreements harder to enforce, fast-growing businesses became 27 to 32% more common in their cities, unless heavy federal regulation in the industry cancelled out the gain.
The Political Economy of State Economic Development Incentives: A Case of Rent Extraction
Russell S. Sobel, Gary A. Wagner, Peter T. Calcagno
Economics & Politics, 2024
Once a state starts handing out ‘megadeal’ business incentives, campaign money flows in and incumbent legislators win by wider margins, which helps explain why these ineffective programs persist.
Does Broadband Affect Local Economic Outcomes Less than We Thought? Micro Evidence from Louisiana
Gary A. Wagner, Hyun Ji Lee
Contemporary Economic Policy, 2024
Faster business internet speeds boost local jobs, start-ups, and start-up survival in Louisiana neighborhoods, but the gains are 60 to 65% smaller than earlier studies suggested.
Regional Innovation Networks & High-Growth Entrepreneurship
Michael E. Araki, Daniel L. Bennett, Gary A. Wagner
Research Policy, 2024
Metro areas where inventors collaborate more closely produce more fast-growing young companies, especially where entrepreneurship is already common.
Does State Tax Reciprocity Affect Interstate Commuting? Evidence from a Natural Experiment
Gary A. Wagner, Jonathan C. Rork
Regional Science and Urban Economics, 2023
When Minnesota and Wisconsin ended their income tax reciprocity agreement, the number of Wisconsin residents commuting to jobs in Minnesota fell by 3 to 5%.
How Does Municipal Governance Structure Affect Innovation and Knowledge Diffusion? Evidence from U.S. Metro Areas
Gary A. Wagner, Timothy M. Komarek
Economics of Governance, 2023
Metro areas with more school districts and special-purpose governments are no less innovative overall, but their innovation is concentrated among fewer inventors and technologies.
Willingness to Pay for Policies to Reduce Health Risks from COVID-19: Evidence from U.S. Professional Sports
Brad R. Humphreys, Gary A. Wagner, John C. Whitehead, Pamela Wicker
Health Economics, 2023
Sports fans placed real dollar value on COVID-19 safety rules at games, but they split sharply: most would pay more for mask requirements, while a smaller group of avid fans would pay less.
Government Contracting, Labor Intensity, and the Local Effects of Fiscal Consolidation: Evidence from the Budget Control Act of 2011
Timothy M. Komarek, Kyle Butts, Gary A. Wagner
Journal of Urban Economics, 2022
When federal contract spending falls, local economies lose jobs faster than they gain them when spending rises — and cuts to labor-intensive industries hit hardest.
Did Video Gaming Expansion Boost Municipal Revenues in Illinois?
Gary A. Wagner, Douglas M. Walker
Southern Economic Journal, 2021
Letting video gambling machines into local bars and restaurants did not raise total city revenues in Illinois; it pulled spending away from other local businesses instead.
Do Cultural Districts Spur Urban Revitalization: Evidence from Louisiana
Javier E. Portillo, Gary A. Wagner
Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization, 2021
Homes inside Louisiana’s state-certified cultural districts sell for about 7% more after certification than similar homes just outside the district lines.
Campaigning for Retirement: State Teacher Union Campaign Contributions and Pension Generosity
Gary A. Wagner, Erick M. Elder
European Journal of Political Economy, 2021
Teachers’ unions that give more to state political campaigns win richer pensions and shift more of the cost to taxpayers, a return of nearly 1,500% on their contributions.
Local Fiscal Adjustments from Depopulation: Evidence from the Post–Cold War Defense Contraction
Timothy M. Komarek, Gary A. Wagner
National Tax Journal, 2021
When the end of the Cold War shrank military towns, local governments held overall budgets steady by raising property taxes, cutting school construction, and borrowing more.

Timothy M. Komarek, Gary A. Wagner
Economics Letters, 2020
The 2011 federal spending caps cost far fewer jobs than predicted, and the losses fell mostly on men and college-educated workers rather than the less-educated workers who usually bear the brunt of downturns.
Patent Intensity and Concentration: The Effect of Institutional Quality on MSA Patent Activity
Gary A. Wagner, Jamie Bologna Pavlik
Papers in Regional Science, 2020
Metro areas with more economic freedom do not necessarily patent more, but their innovation is spread across many more inventors and technologies.
